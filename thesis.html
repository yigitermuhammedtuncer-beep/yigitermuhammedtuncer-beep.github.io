3D Reconstruction of Parametric CAD Models from 2D Engineering Drawings

Feb 2026 – Nov 2026 · Ongoing

Given only a 2D technical drawing, the goal is for a computer to independently reconstruct it into an editable, parametric 3D CAD model — automating a process that currently takes engineers hours or days for every legacy drawing sitting in a factory archive.

Why this matters

Factories and engineering offices hold millions of legacy 2D technical drawings (paper, DXF, PDF). Converting them to 3D today requires an engineer to manually redraw them in SolidWorks or FreeCAD. This thesis designs a modular hybrid deep learning pipeline that automates that conversion for a well-defined class of mechanical parts, while staying honest about where it currently breaks down.

Pipeline architecture

1

Vision & Perception — Seeing + Reading

Detects orthographic views and dimension callouts (YOLOv11), converts them into clean vector primitives without thinning artifacts (U-Net + Transformer), builds a topology graph (GraphSAGE), and extracts tolerances via OCR.

↓
2

Translational Bridge

A small trainable adapter (MLP) maps the topology graph embedding into the prefix space of a frozen, pre-trained CAD decoder — avoiding the need to train a giant network from scratch on limited hardware.

↓
3

Sequential CAD Synthesis

The frozen decoder generates an ordered sequence of parametric CAD operations — sketch, extrude, revolve — mirroring how a human engineer builds a feature tree.

↓
4

Quality Control & Verification

Generated dimensions are cross-validated against the OCR-extracted ground truth from Phase 1. Discrepancies are corrected before the model is dispatched to the FreeCAD API as an editable, parametric .FCStd solid.

Scope

In scope

  • Single, non-assembly, prismatic parts
  • Sketch + Extrude operations
  • Sketch + Revolve operations
  • Unification of the two above

Out of scope

  • Multi-part assemblies
  • Sweep operations (e.g. a pipe elbow)
  • Loft operations (e.g. an airplane wing)
  • — reserved for future work —

Research questions

RQ1 — Vectorization fidelity

When a deep-vectorization architecture is evaluated on drawings dense with GD&T/tolerance annotations, to what extent is intersection-point fidelity preserved compared to traditional thinning methods, which are known to corrupt corners and propagate errors downstream?

RQ2 — Cross-view fusion

Does projection-aligned cross-attention fusion across orthographic views (front/top/side) meaningfully outperform naive aggregation (concatenation + MLP) for 3D reconstruction accuracy — adapting the epipolar-attention idea from 3D vision to an orthographic, graph-based setting no prior work has tested?

RQ3 — Prefix-tuning generalization (core question)

Does prefix-tuning, known to steer large general-purpose frozen models (GPT-2, CLIP), still work when the frozen model is a decoder trained exclusively on a narrow, symbolic CAD-command vocabulary — or is that steerability an emergent property of scale and training diversity that a narrow-domain decoder simply doesn't have?

RQ4 — Hallucination correction

When a generated dimension is cross-validated against an independently OCR-extracted tolerance value through rule-based correction middleware, to what extent can numerical hallucinations be detected and corrected within acceptable engineering tolerance bounds?

Where this sits in the literature

Classical / rule-based systems (GTX 5000, AVSED — 1990s): 2D vectorization only, no 3D reconstruction, and thinning algorithms distort geometry at intersections.
Modern end-to-end generative systems (DeepCAD, eCAD-Net, CMT): generate 3D B-Rep, but assume a 3D input already exists, need massive GPU clusters, or rely on pixel-dense multi-view CNNs.
Graph-based 2D analysis systems (GraphSAGE-based entity classification, Deep Vectorization): state-of-the-art at parsing 2D topology, but never bridge into 3D CAD generation.

This thesis connects the graph-based 2D analysis cluster to the lightweight, frozen-backbone generation cluster — a bridge no reviewed paper has built.
